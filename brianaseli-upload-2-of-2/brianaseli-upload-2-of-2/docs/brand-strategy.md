# Brand strategy — Brian Aseli, The Most Bankable Host

## 1. The job the brand has to do

A corporate events lead has three or four names on a shortlist and twenty minutes to decide. They are not asking "is he famous?" They are asking:

- Will he make our CEO and our speakers look good?
- Will he keep a long programme on time without it feeling rushed?
- Can he handle a serious panel *and* a celebration in the same day?
- Is he easy to work with before the event?

Brian's public record answers the first three better than most hosts can: nine years of live national television and radio since 2017, daily four-hour live radio, late-night flagship TV, awards-night hosting, and a theatre practice that won a national award for musical arrangement. The brand's job is to translate that broadcast record into **event-buyer language** — and then make enquiring effortless.

## 2. Positioning

**For** organisations running events where the room matters — conferences, awards nights, launches, executive conversations —
**Brian Aseli** is the Nairobi host who brings
**the discipline of live broadcasting and the timing of the stage**,
**so that** the programme runs to time, speakers are set up to land, and the audience leaves remembering the event, not the gaps in it.

### Why "bankable" is earned, not claimed

"The Most Bankable Host" is already in public use — NTV used it in 2023 and 2025 headlines and Nation Media Group used it in a 2025 staff spotlight. We keep it as the signature line, but never as a ranking claim ("best in Africa"). On the site, *bankable* means **reliable return on a booking**: he turns up prepared, he keeps time, he lifts the room. Every page is built to prove that, not to repeat it.

## 3. Message house

**Headline (final):** *He reads the room. Then he leads it.*

Short, physical, and true to the two things buyers pay a host for — audience awareness and control. It improves on the brief's starting line ("The room has a rhythm…") by putting Brian, not the room, at the centre, and by making the order of operations clear: listen first, then lead.

**Supporting line:** Conferences, awards nights, launches and high-stakes conversations — hosted with the discipline of live broadcasting and the timing of the stage.

**Signature:** The Most Bankable Host. **Sign-off:** *I host. I inspire. I leave a memory.* (kept from his own materials, used once per page, in the footer and on the booking page.)

## 5. Service architecture

Ordered by commercial value and evidence strength:

1. **Corporate & Conference Hosting** — plenaries, summits, AGMs, leadership forums
2. **Master of Ceremonies** — galas, awards, launches, institutional functions
3. **Moderation & Facilitation** — panels, fireside chats, roundtables, public dialogues
4. **Entertainment & Brand Experiences** — activations, showcases, festivals
5. **Broadcast & Interview Hosting** — selected projects, subject to employer policy
6. **Speaking & Creative** — storytelling and communication talks; creative direction and scripting (from his bio's "available for" list; confirm before launch)

Services are presented like a product range on the Zero Motorcycles reference — a tabbed "range" on the homepage that leads into detail sections — but each service is described as *client need → what Brian does → what that makes possible*, with evidence pulled automatically from the verified archive.

## 6. Visual identity, revision 3 (18 October 2026)

- **Voice:** all site copy is now first person ("I read the room. Then I lead it.", "Why organisers book me"). Bios stay third person because organisers read them aloud.
- **No em dashes** in site copy or the media kit.
- **Hero, Tecno-style:** four cinematic slides (the room, the show, the conversation, the voice). The background camera drifts, follow-spots sweep, dust floats in the light, and I step into frame. Headline lines rise in, dash pagination fills with progress, and the slides respond to swipe and pointer depth. Mobile text is smaller and sits under the photo.
- **Imagery:** the stage-lighting images are now section backgrounds across the site, fading into each other with slow parallax. Cards carry real photos instead: my portraits on the service cards, real YouTube thumbnails on the video cards, real event photos on the credits. Every card photo is shown in the brand blue tone, and its true colour comes through on hover.
- **Motion:** headlines rise word by word, and sections fade, lift and un-blur as they arrive. "Why organisers book me" opens with an iris reveal: each strength's lamp flickers on, a light sweeps its title, and then the spotlight moves between strengths on its own until the visitor takes over. All motion respects reduced-motion settings.

## 6b. Visual identity, revision 2 (October 2026)

After review, the first build was judged too flat and too CV-like. Revision 2 changes direction:

- **Concept: the stage.** Every dark section is lit like a stage — the supplied blue stage-lighting backgrounds, CSS light beams, haze and fine grain — with Brian cut out from his studio portraits and placed *in* the light. The site feels like a show, not a résumé.
- **Light and dark mode.** The "room" (reading sections) switches between a cool off-white and deep navy; it follows the visitor's system setting and remembers a manual choice. The "stage" sections stay dark in both modes, because a stage is always dark.
- **Palette:** stage navy `#050B1C`, beam blue `#3D7BFF`, copper `#F07832` (calls to action, the follow-spot dot), ivory `#F4F0E7`, gold `#D4B06A` (sign-off only). Copper text on light backgrounds uses `#B5500F` for contrast.
- **Wordmark:** "Brian Aseli" set in Sora with a glowing copper follow-spot dot. The BA monogram is retired.
- **Motion, all user-controllable:** a three-act hero slideshow (pause button, pauses on focus, off with reduced motion); a follow-spot that tracks the pointer; a role marquee with a pause control; drag-and-swipe carousels for services and videos; a pinned photo story on About; an oversized "Brian Aseli" footer wordmark lit by the pointer.
- **Media-house restraint:** the site leads with Brian, not his employers. Station names appear only where a reader needs them (a source link, or naming the show he hosts now). Career-move news about joining or leaving stations is kept in the research register but off the site. The on-air history table was replaced with "The shows he hosts" (current shows only).

## 6a. Visual identity — original (revision 1, superseded)

### Colour — the decision

Brian's existing materials split in two: the bio uses **forest green, sage and gold** ("refined, modern, iconic"), the booking poster uses **deep blue, electric blue and orange** ("energy, clarity, humour").

**Recommendation: navy and copper as the primary system; green retired from the website.**

- The poster is the asset that already carries his **booking number** — it is his commercial face. Navy + orange is the system buyers have already seen.
- Navy reads as *stage lighting and broadcast studio* — cinematic, and serious enough for a bank's AGM. Forest green reads *lifestyle and fashion*, which undersells the corporate offer.
- Copper on navy gives the energy of the poster without the poster's busyness.
- Green can remain in lifestyle content (e.g., Instagram) without conflict.

|---|---|---|---|
| Midnight navy | `#071A33` | Cinematic sections, header, footer | Ivory on navy 15.3:1 |
| Deep navy | `#040D1C` | Hero depth, photo shadows | — |
| Warm ivory | `#F4F0E7` | Editorial pages, reading surfaces | Navy on ivory 15.3:1 |
| Copper | `#F07832` | CTAs (navy text on copper 6.2:1), accents on navy (6.2:1) | **Never** text on ivory (2.5:1) |

| Mist | `#B9C1CF` | Secondary text on navy | 9.6:1 |
| Slate | `#4A5366` | Secondary text on ivory | 6.8:1 |

### Photography treatment

The supplied studio portraits are black-and-white. They are rendered as a **navy-to-ivory duotone** so they sit inside the palette without retouching the subject. Colour is reserved for real-life moments (the event photograph, the leather-jacket editorial) — colour signals "this happened." No stock imagery, no generated images of Brian. Employer-branded images (e.g., *The Trend* poster) and the timeline graphic are not used.

### Typography

- **Sora** (variable, 300–700): display headlines, numerals, navigation. Geometric, broadcast-graphic feel; tight tracking at large sizes.
- **Manrope** (variable, 400–700): body, forms, labels. Highly legible at small sizes on both backgrounds.
- **Instrument Serif Italic**: used *only* for pull quotes and the sign-off — one editorial voice, never for UI.

All three are self-hosted (SIL Open Font License) — no third-party font requests.

### Motion

Restrained: reveal-on-scroll fades (16px, 500ms), a slow marquee of roles under the hero, tab transitions. All disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 7. Tone of voice

Confident, warm, specific. Write like a seasoned producer briefing a client, not a hype reel.

- **Do:** "He keeps the run-of-show honest." "Panellists arrive briefed; the audience hears a conversation, not a recital."
- **Don't:** "world-class", "unparalleled", "bringing dreams to life", "Africa's number one".
- Third person for credibility; first person only in the sign-off and in attributed quotes from his own bio.
- Employer names appear as facts of his career, never as endorsements; no logos.

## 8. Independence safeguards

- Footer and booking page: bookings are made directly with Brian; not engagements of, or endorsed by, any media house; programme and station names used for identification only.
- Broadcast & interview hosting service carries "subject to employer policy".
- NMG-organised events are held back until Brian confirms which were independent bookings.
